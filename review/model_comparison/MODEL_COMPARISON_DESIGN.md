## Question and scope

Can global controller skill plus family difficulty predict repeated correctness adequately, or do known coefficient coordinates predict family-dependent response beyond that explanation? If coordinates help, does that benefit depend on their actual assignment to controllers, and is it stronger in the original orientation than in either of the two fixed random orientations?

Use the existing pilot: 3 orientations × 60 families × 12 common-qualified controllers × 8 rollouts = 17,280 steered binary observations. The other 480 evaluation observations are contemporaneous baseline, reported as existing context only; exclude them from all candidate fitting and primary losses. Do not interpret baseline as a thirteenth coefficient vector at the same dose. All outcomes were previously exposed; computationally isolated folds do not make this confirmatory. The estimand is conditional on this selected bank, these exposed families, frozen dose, model and two random orientations. The pilot's specificity conclusion remains inconclusive.

The PR review of 2026-09-09, the published closeout, and `../geometry_specificity/LOCAL_RESPONSE_AND_PILOT_DRAFT.md` motivate this comparison. No new literature claim is needed: the statistical choices below are explicit definitions and elementary likelihood calculations. No claim of novelty, calibrated significance or semantic mechanism follows from rejecting a simple null.

## Minimum candidates

Fit each orientation separately; no borrowing of labels across orientations. Let Y_ikr be correctness including invalid answers as zero, c_k the frozen unit rank-eight coefficient vector, and sigma the logistic function. Retain original coordinate scale; do not standardize, whiten, select axes or estimate a distance metric using outcomes.

| Model | Logit of success probability | Role |
|---|---|---|
| F: family difficulty | alpha_i | Family-only baseline, constant across controllers |
| R: additive Rasch | alpha_i + theta_k | Global controller ordering plus family difficulty |
| L: local coefficient response | alpha_i + beta_i^T c_k | Family-dependent linear logit response with eight slopes per family |
| P: permuted-coordinate response | alpha_i + beta_i^T c_pi(k) | One nongeometric assignment comparator, with exactly L's architecture and penalty |

L is deliberately a **nonnested alternative** to R, not R plus hundreds of extra free controller and interaction terms. Its slopes can encode both a common coefficient-linked skill component and family deviations; no additional global slope is fitted because it would duplicate those slopes. A predictive gain over R supports coefficient-linked response; it does not by itself isolate crossed specialization from smooth global skill. Report whether fitted family slopes produce reversals descriptively. There is no free 8×8 covariance fit, quadratic term, latent factor model, kernel search or model zoo. L has 540 coefficients per orientation (60 intercepts plus 480 slopes); R has 71 and F 60. This is a substantive small-controller limitation despite 5,760 observations per orientation.

P is necessary here to distinguish coordinates' actual mapping from extra capacity. Use exactly one outcome-independent permutation of the 12 retained positions, shared across orientations and folds: NumPy PCG64 with seed 20260911, `Generator(PCG64(20260911)).permutation(12)`. Freeze its explicit list before fitting. Do not redraw fixed points or search permutations. It preserves the multiset of coefficient vectors and the spectrum of the full Gram matrix while breaking controller assignment; it does not preserve each labeled pair distance. P has the same coefficient availability as L at prediction time and the same full-panel complexity, but fold-specific conditioning may differ; report design ranks/condition numbers. One permutation is a comparator, not a permutation-test null distribution. Orthogonal rotation would preserve L's ridge predictions and therefore is not a useful nongeometric comparator.

## Identification, regularization and optimization

There is no separate global intercept. Each alpha_i is an absolute family logit. For R constrain sum(theta_k)=0 over the **training** controllers, implemented with an orthonormal contrast basis; penalize its contrast coefficients, equivalently the squared norm of centered theta. No sum constraint applies to alpha or beta. L/P contain no policy intercepts. All penalty directions are positive, making the finite design objective strictly convex even when the design is rank deficient or outcomes separate.

Minimize the summed binomial negative log likelihood plus

`0.5 * [0.01 * sum_i(alpha_i^2) + lambda * sum(extra_coefficients^2)]`.

For F there are no extra coefficients. Primary lambda=1; exactly two sensitivity fits use lambda=0.25 and 4. Intercept penalty stays 0.01 everywhere. These are weakly regularized absolute logits around success probability 0.5, not estimated hierarchical priors. The objective is a **sum**, not a mean: keep this normalization unchanged across folds and models. No tuning, early stopping selected by test loss, empirical-Bayes shrinkage or winner chosen from sensitivities. Stronger regularization does not create information about unseen identities. Report primary and both sensitivities side by side; sensitivity reversals imply fragility.

Aggregate training successes and trial counts by family/controller only after applying the split mask. Use float64, stable `logaddexp(0,eta)`, and stable sigmoid. Fit by damped Newton with analytic gradient X^T(np-y)+D b and Hessian X^T diag(np(1-p)) X+D. Start zero; Cholesky or direct linear solve, never matrix inverse. Backtrack step sizes 1, 1/2, ... up to 40 attempts with Armijo constant 1e-4. Stop at max absolute gradient / total training trials < 1e-9; cap 100 Newton steps. Save iterations, objective, gradient, finite parameters and line-search status. If solve or convergence fails, mark the affected model/split failed and comparison incomplete; do not replace it with another optimizer after viewing losses. Family-block solves may implement L/P efficiently but must agree with the generic synthetic solver. No model loading is involved.

## Splits and what they identify

Freeze controller order from `common_safe_indices`, family order from `PREPARED.json`, and zero-based rollout indices from the sealed schedule. Apply the same split to all models and orientations.

1. **Known family / known controller, new rollout (primary adequacy diagnostic).** Four outer folds hold rollout pairs {0,1}, {2,3}, {4,5}, {6,7} across *every* family, controller and orientation. Train on the remaining six. Each row is scored once; all controller intercepts are available for R. This tests repeated prediction on the fixed cells. It cannot establish extrapolation to new controllers, new families or prospective independence from earlier scientific inspection.
2. **Known family / held-out controller (primary coordinate-transfer diagnostic).** Twelve leave-one-coefficient-position-out folds, omitting that position in every orientation and all eight rollouts. Train each orientation on its other 11 controllers and evaluate the omitted controller. R's omitted theta is unavailable: explicitly predict alpha_i + 0, the training centered-skill reference. F likewise uses alpha_i. L/P use the omitted controller's known assigned covariates. No outcomes of that controller, even from another family or orientation, may calibrate its intercept. This compares covariate-based transfer with a declared generic fallback; R is not claimed to estimate an unseen controller's true skill. L's win here alone can reflect smooth global skill. Read it together with split 1 and descriptive slope variation/crossings.

Evaluation seeds were designed independently by condition, unlike shared calibration seeds. Verify that assertion against the sealed schedule, not just the function comment. Calibration data never enter the study. Block entire rollout indices in split 1 conservatively even if seeds are independent. Before fitting, map repeated seeds/coupled generation identifiers: if any dependency crosses planned train/test boundaries, stop for supervisor redesign and freeze the amendment; do not quietly ignore it or split coupled observations. Family/prompt dependencies persist across folds by design. Same-family results across controllers are not independent samples. No rowwise random CV.

Family-heldout prediction is **not included**: these models have no features identifying a new family's alpha or slopes. A future explicitly calibrated-family design could use some outcomes on that new family to estimate them and test the rest, but would be a different, labeled-calibration estimand. Predicting simultaneously unseen families/controllers without covariates or calibration is not identifiable. No zero-filled family effect is presented as learned generalization. Folds are overlapping training sets, not independent replications.

## Primary loss, paired aggregation and descriptive checks

Primary metric: held-out Bernoulli log loss in nats per observation. Before scoring, clip every prediction to [1e-6, 1-1e-6] with the same fixed rule for all models, and disclose the count clipped per model/split. This is numerical truncation of the proper log score; strictly proper behavior applies inside the allowed interval, not at arbitrarily extreme true probabilities. Do not optimize clipped loss. No post hoc threshold tuning or accuracy-based model selection.

For each split generate one out-of-fold prediction per steered observation. Average row losses over eight rollouts and twelve controllers **within family**, then average the 60 family means, separately per orientation. Differences are computed on identical observations before aggregation: gains R−L, F−R, and P−L (positive favors the second named model). Report all three orientation gains and original-minus-each-random gain differences; the mean-random contrast is supplementary. Retain the 12 controller-specific mean gains as descriptive diagnostics, not 12 independent replications. Averages over folds are unnecessary because each observation is scored once.

Report family paired gain median, mean, range and fraction positive, and the four rollout-block mean gains for split 1. No t test over folds or controller pairs, no bootstrap confidence interval borrowed from the pilot, no claims of nominal coverage. These are finite-panel descriptive prediction contrasts; uncertainty about new task populations remains unresolved. Predefine 0.005 nats/observation as a practical discussion threshold, not a statistically justified detectable effect or significance cutoff. A stable candidate benefit requires primary gains above that threshold for R−L and P−L in both splits, with both sensitivity signs positive. This is a supervisor decision aid, never a semantic-specificity classifier.

Residuals and crossings are secondary. On split-1 out-of-fold predictions, summarize squared doubly centered observed cell means and residual cell means, explicitly noting that residual energy is noisy and fitted probabilities from overlapping training folds are dependent. Descriptively compare rollout pairs 0–3 versus 4–7 for empirical ranking reversals and fitted L rankings; never treat the 66 controller pairs as independent evidence or select interesting families. Raw probability-scale interaction is insufficient: for p_ik=sigma(alpha_i+theta_k), strictly increasing sigma preserves every controller ordering, while double centering generally leaves nonzero interaction. The synthetic example demonstrates this directly. Link-scale residual improvement is not proof of a semantic mechanism either.

## Measurement limits and finite sensitivity

Eight Bernoulli trials per cell are noisy. The retained bank was conditioned on common qualification; controllers need not span directions well when one is held out (only 11 remain for eight slopes plus intercept). Record coefficient rank and singular values per training fold, without outcome-based removal. With 12 controllers, local extrapolation may be shrinkage-dominated and exact latent slopes poorly identified. A failed predictive gain cannot exclude real weak slope structure.

The existing closeout reports different response scales and S/E across orientations; equal dose and token activity did not equalize information. Do not divide predictive gains by estimated S/E or deattenuate correlations using a noisy plug-in ratio. The fixed synthetic attenuation check reports expected and observed centered energy at four and eight repeats for a Rasch probability matrix. It illustrates measurement contamination and does not calibrate the original pilot's confidence intervals. The only real-fit sensitivity is the two prespecified penalties; existing four held-rollout blocks already provide finite temporal/block diagnostics. No repeated repartitioning, power-driven sample expansion, extra null orientations or indefinite tuning follows.
